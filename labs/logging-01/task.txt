OBJECTIVE
  Filter the systemd journal by identifier, priority, output format
  and order, and save the results to files.

PREREQUISITES
  - Work as your normal user, {{LAB_USER}}. Reading the whole journal
    may require sudo, depending on your group membership.

TASKS
  The journal contains 8 test entries with the syslog identifier
  labjournal, written when the lab started. They have the
  priorities crit, err, warning, notice and info. Save the results
  in the directory journal-lab in your home directory. The files
  must belong to your user.

  1. Create all.txt with every entry of identifier labjournal in
     the default output format. It holds all 8 entries and nothing
     from other identifiers.
  2. Create errors.txt with the labjournal entries of priority err
     or more severe. It holds 3 entries.
  3. Create warnings.txt with the labjournal entries of priority
     warning or more severe. It holds 5 entries.
  4. Create latest.txt with only the 3 newest labjournal entries,
     the newest one first.
  5. Create errors.json with the same 3 entries as errors.txt in
     JSON format, one entry per line.

PRACTICE (not graded)
  - Show the entries of the unit systemd-logind.
  - Show only the messages of the current boot.
  - Show the entries of the last 10 minutes, and combine this with
    a unit and a priority.
  - Follow the journal in real time and leave the view again.

NOTES
  - The journal keeps entries of earlier lab runs. Select by
    identifier and priority; do not edit the files by hand.
  - Priorities from most to least severe: emerg, alert, crit, err,
    warning, notice, info, debug.
  - Reference: man journalctl and man systemd.journal-fields.

GRADING
  labctl grade logging-01
